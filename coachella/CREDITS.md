# Image Credits

Every image used in this zine is in the **public domain** or has been
explicitly **dedicated to the public domain** by its photographer. Each entry
below links back to its catalog record at the Library of Congress so any future
reader can verify the rights statement themselves.

## Source 1 — Farm Security Administration / Office of War Information Collection

> "The contents of the Library of Congress Farm Security Administration / Office
> of War Information Black-and-White Negatives are in the public domain and are
> free to use and reuse."
> — *Library of Congress, About this Collection*

These photographs were made by US federal employees in the course of their
official duties (1935–1944) and are therefore in the public domain in the
United States with no known restrictions on publication.

A note on **Imperial Valley** photos: several of Lange's images included here
were taken in the Imperial Valley, immediately south of the Coachella Valley.
Paiz himself describes these two valleys as adjacent and connected — in
*The Strikers of Coachella* (Ch. 1, p. 25), he writes of farmworkers driving
toward the valley "twinkles of the Salton Sea, the large body of water that
separates the Coachella Valley from its larger, southern cousin, the Imperial
Valley." The same labor, the same desert, the same period.

A note on the **UFW eagle**: the official UFW eagle/aguila logo is both
trademarked and copyrighted by the United Farm Workers of America, and the
union actively enforces both. This zine therefore does **not** use the clean
trademark version. Where the eagle appears in this zine, it is part of a
larger document explicitly marked Public Domain on the Internet Archive (the
csfst collection above) — the eagle as it appears in those original 1966–1967
documents, not extracted as a standalone logo.

The page images above were extracted from the access PDFs at 150 dpi using
`pdftoppm`. The original PDFs and the rights statements are at the IA item
URLs in the table.

## What is *not* used

This zine deliberately does **not** use:

- The photographs from the *El Malcriado* spread that opens Paiz's book
  (credited to Bob Thurber, Walter P. Reuther Library, Wayne State University —
  not in the public domain).
- Any photographs of César Chávez, the UFW eagle/aguila, or any UFW publication
  imagery for which the rights are held by the United Farm Workers, the Reuther
  Library, or any other rightsholder.
- Press-agency photographs (AP, Getty, etc.).
- Any AI-generated imagery.

The eagle motif on the cover and on the *Sí Se Puede* page is a stepped,
Aztec-inspired drawing made from scratch in SVG for this project. It is not the
UFW's mark.
